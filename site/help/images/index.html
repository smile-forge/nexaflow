Images

Opening pictures

  • Double-click a picture in the File System page. PNG, JPEG, GIF, BMP, ICO, TIFF and WebP open here.
  • Open several as one set: select them and choose As Image.
  • A picture inside an archive opens the same way.
  • Where a folder holds a fair share of pictures, its actions offer Slideshow and Album, taking that folder's pictures in name order.
  • A picture that cannot be read says so in place of the picture.
  • A JPEG or TIFF taken on its side is shown upright, the way its camera recorded it.
  • The tab is named for the picture you opened, or counts the set, and the breadcrumb names the folder. Show me

Views

When a tab holds more than one image, pick a view from the switch at the top left. The picture you were on stays current when you switch. Show me

  • Carousel shows one image at a time, and is where a tab opens unless something asked for another view.
  • Album shows thumbnails with file names. Click one to make it current, double-click to open it in the carousel.
  • Explore shows thumbnails beside the selected picture, with a draggable divider.
  • Collage scatters the whole set on a canvas.

Moving through a set

  • Step back and forth in the carousel with the arrows either side, with Space, or with the left and right arrow keys. The arrow keys and the mouse wheel move between pictures in the carousel only. Navigation does not loop, and the arrows disappear at the first and last picture.
  • The wheel steps to the next picture, except at actual size, where it pans instead.
  • Jump to a picture: click its dot beneath the carousel. A long set shows twenty dots at a time, with arrows for the rest.

Details

  • Details at the top left, or I, opens a panel beside the picture with what its file says about it: its name, type, size and dimensions; the camera and lens; the exposure — aperture, shutter speed, ISO, focal length and flash; when it was taken, by the camera's clock; where, from its GPS position; its description, author and copyright; and its colour profile. Show me
  • It follows the picture you are on, in every view and in the editor, and shows only what the file holds: a screenshot, or a picture saved without its details, has its file and nothing more. An edited picture also says the size it will be saved at.
  • What it shows is what Save keeps, unless you choose in the save options to remove it.

Slideshow and full screen

  • Advance on a timer: turn on Auto. It loops back to the first picture at the end and runs in whichever view you are in. Show me
  • Set the speed with the slider: slow, medium or fast — a picture every ten seconds, four seconds, or one. A change takes effect straight away.
  • The slideshow stops when you move to another tab and resumes when you come back.
  • Fill the display: View full screen. Move forward with Space, the right or down arrow, or a click; back with the left or up arrow, or a right-click. The wheel steps too.
  • Esc returns to the tab on the picture you stopped at and stops the slideshow. A slideshow started before you went full screen keeps running, and every picture is shown with its edits.

Rotating, flipping, scaling and the collage

  • Rotate in ninety-degree steps, flip across or upside down, or switch between fit and actual size, from the toolbar floating over the picture in the carousel and in Explore. At actual size the picture scrolls. Show me
  • A turn or a flip is an edit: it is remembered with the picture and undone with Ctrl+Z, like everything in the editor below. Fit and actual size change only the view.
  • In the collage, drag anywhere to pan and use the wheel to zoom about the pointer. The chips in the corner offer minus, plus, Fit and 1:1.
  • An overview appears in the bottom right once the pile runs past the edges; drag inside it to move the view. Collage positions are kept when you leave and come back. Double-click a picture to open it in the carousel.

Editing

Open the editor with Edit at the top right, with E, or from Edit among a picture's actions in the File System page. The picture sits on a canvas you can pan and zoom, with six tools beside it in the order a picture is usually worked on — Crop, Adjust, Retouch, Cut out, Markup and Resize — each saying at the head of its panel what it is for. Show me

  • Edits never touch the file until you save. They are kept beside the picture in a hidden file named after it, and every view shows the picture with them applied — the carousel, the thumbnails, full screen, and what the assistant is shown. A picture with edits is marked edited beside its name.
  • Crop shows the whole picture with a frame over it. Drag the corners or edges, or drag inside to move it; it stops at the edge of the picture. Choose a shape — free, the picture's own, square, 4:3 or 16:9 — and flip it upright with Portrait. A rule-of-thirds grid helps placing. Enter applies the crop; Esc puts it back as it was when you opened the crop tool. Show me
  • Straighten with the slider, or turn on Level line and drag along something that should be level — a horizon, a shelf — or upright, and the picture turns to match. The crop shrinks to keep empty corners out of the picture, and grows back if you turn back. Show me
  • Adjust opens on the quick fixes: Auto enhance reads the picture and sets the sliders for you, so any of them can still be changed after. Below them are the light — exposure, contrast, highlights and shadows — and the colour — saturation, temperature and tint. Double-click a slider to reset it, or Reset them all. Show me
  • AI enhance cleans the picture up — softness, noise and JPEG blocks — with the picture tools on this machine, and repairs the faces in it as it goes; Strength mixes it with the picture as it was. The first time, the tools are a download of about 640 MB, offered in the banner above the picture. An enhanced picture is made once and kept, so coming back to it is instant. Show me
  • Markup draws arrows, boxes, text, pen lines and highlighter. Pick a tool and drag on the picture; for text, click where it goes and type in the panel. Select picks an annotation to move it, restyle it or delete it with Del. Annotations stay on what they mark if you crop again later. Show me
  • Cut out picks something out of the picture with picture selection on this machine: click the thing to keep, right-click (or Alt-click) what to leave out, or drag a box round it — or type what it is, like “mug” or “child in a red vest”, and press Find to pick out every one. Copy puts it on the clipboard without its background, and Save cut-out saves it as a PNG, transparent around it and cropped to it; the picture itself is untouched. The first time, picture selection is a download of about 1.8 GB, offered above the picture. Show me Remove background keeps what is picked in the picture itself and makes everything else transparent — an edit like any other, undone or put back with Keep background. A JPEG can't be transparent, so it is then saved as a copy, offered as a PNG. Show me
    • Retouch rubs things out of the picture — scratches, dust, wires, people, signs — and fills where they were. Mark what to erase with Pick, which picks a thing as Cut out does, or with Brush: brush over it, and right-drag to take marking back. Anything picked is erased with what you brush, so pick a person and brush their shadow. Then choose how to fill it: AI fills from what is round it and is best where an edge or shape runs through what you erase; the first time it is a download of about 210 MB, offered above the picture. Content-aware copies the picture's own texture in, sharp on grass, walls, road, sky and fabric, and needs nothing downloaded. Heal copies texture from where you Alt-click, matched to the light and colour round what you heal. Erase makes it one step of your edits — undone like any other — and a picture restored with AI enhance is restored as erased. Show me
  • Tools that need a model this PC hasn't downloaded yet — picking in Cut out and Retouch, AI fill, AI enhance and AI upscale — are greyed out until it is: hover one to see what it needs and how big the download is, and the bar above the picture offers the download while its panel is open. Until then Retouch opens on Brush and Content-aware, which need nothing. Show me
  • Resize sets the size the picture is saved at, as a percentage or in pixels, keeping its proportions unless you say otherwise. Show me
  • AI upscale makes the picture twice or four times its size with the picture tools, restoring detail rather than stretching it, and turns AI enhance on. Crops and annotations stay where they were. A picture too large to make four times its size is offered twice only. Show me
  • Save options, beside Save as copy, choose whether saving keeps the camera details, location, dates and descriptions, or removes them, and set the quality of a JPEG or WebP. The colour profile is always kept. A dot on the button means Save has something to tell you — why this picture can only be saved as a copy, or what saving it will drop — said inside. Show me
  • Undo and Redo step through everything you did to the picture while it has been open — a slider drag, a crop drag or a pen line counts as one step. Before shows the picture as it is on disk, to compare. Revert removes every edit, and can itself be undone. Show me
  • Save writes the edits into the picture, after asking: the original pixels are replaced, the picture is saved upright, and its camera details are kept unless you chose to remove them. Save as copy writes a new file in the format its name asks for and leaves the original and its edits as they are. A picture inside an archive, an animated GIF or WebP, or an icon can only be saved as a copy. Show me
  • Done, or Esc, leaves the editor; the edits stay. Ctrl+Z and Ctrl+Y undo and redo, and Ctrl+S and Ctrl+Shift+S save, while editing. Show me
  • In the File System page a picture's edits go wherever it goes: copying, moving, renaming and deleting it take them along, a copy that is renamed to avoid a clash keeps its own, and dragging it to another program brings them too. Revert edits appears among the actions of an edited picture.
  • If a picture is changed by another program after you edited it, the viewer asks whether to keep your edits on the new picture or discard them. A picture inside an archive can be edited, but its edits last only while it is open — save a copy to keep them.

Converting a page to Markdown

  • A picture of a page of writing — a scan, a photographed page, a form, a receipt — can be converted to Markdown. Convert to Markdown sits at the top right whenever the picture is one; until page reading is on this PC every picture offers it, with the download. Show me
  • A tinted line above the picture says how far the reading has got. Stop asks first: Show what's converted opens what has been read so far, Discard lets it go, and Keep converting carries on. Once it is read, the Markdown opens in the Markdown editor in the right-hand pane: columns as one text, headings, formulas as LaTeX, tables, and the page's figures cut out of it. A figure the workspace's image model can draw again as a diagram comes back as one.
  • It reads the picture as you see it, edits and all, at its full size: crop to the page or straighten it first and it reads better. The reading is kept, so converting the same picture again opens at once.
  • The Markdown is a draft to edit as you like. Its first save asks where to keep it — a Markdown Package, with its figures inside it, unless you pick a Markdown file.
  • Page reading runs on this PC. The first time it is a download of about 4 GB, offered above the picture. Show me

Deleting and the assistant

  • Delete a picture: right-click it or its thumbnail and choose Delete. It asks first, sends the file and its edits to the Recycle Bin and removes it from every view; the selection moves to what is left.
  • The assistant can look at the picture you are on, as edited, and answer questions about what is in it. It can report the format, dimensions and file size, and step to the next or previous picture.
  • The assistant works on the picture you are on, and nothing else: a picture is its to see or change only once you have it open.
  • Ask the assistant to pick something out — “select the dog”, “find the woman with red hair” — and it opens the editor's Cut out with every one it found picked, ready to copy or save. Ask it to cut something out and it opens the cut-out in a new tab for you as a draft: Save as keeps it where you choose, starting beside the picture.
  • A picture made for you — drawn by the assistant with nowhere asked to save it, or cut out — opens as a draft, with a strip over it saying it is not saved yet. Save as keeps it, with any edits you made to it, and the tab becomes that file; a draft you never save is kept only until Windows clears its temporary files. Show me
  • Ask the assistant to clean up or enlarge the picture, or to erase something from it — “remove the sign”, “get rid of the people in the background” — and it does it as one of your edits in the editor, which you can undo or save.
  • Ask the assistant to read a page — “what does this letter say”, “type up this receipt” — and it reads the picture with page reading, its text opening beside it.
  • In a conversation, this tab's context chip previews every image it holds.
